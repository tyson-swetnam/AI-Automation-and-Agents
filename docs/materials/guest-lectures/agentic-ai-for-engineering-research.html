Three old ideas, still the right questions

Sequence, selection, iteration

Sequence

Do this, then this. Find sources, extract values, build the table, write the summary.

Selection

If this, do that. Is the value in the source? If not, mark it and look again.

Iteration

Repeat until done. Draft, check, revise, until the check passes or a person says stop.

Böhm and Jacopini showed in 1966 that a few control structures are enough for any flow diagram[3]. In an agent, the model decides the selections and when the iteration stops[4].

A design choice, not a buzzword

Workflow or agent?

Workflow

You fix the path

“Systems where LLMs and tools are orchestrated through predefined code paths”[1]. Predictable, easy to test. Your hazard assessment today is one.

Agent

The model picks the path

“Systems where LLMs dynamically direct their own processes and tool usage”[1]. Flexible, harder to predict and to verify.

Engineer’s rule of thumb: use the simplest design that does the job, and add autonomy only where it pays for its failure modes.

Grounding: hand the model the information

What it absorbed in training

Parametric memory

Broad, but frozen, unsourced and sometimes wrong. It cannot tell you where a number came from.

What you put in front of it

Retrieved context

Your sources, pasted or retrieved into the prompt. Retrieval-augmented models wrote more specific and more factual text than a model working from memory alone[8].

In practice: paste a source pack, attach files to a project, or connect a data source. Today you will paste one.

Verification

A model checking itself is not a check

Self-feedback can help

Self-Refine: the same model critiques and revises its own output, about 20 points (absolute) better on average across its seven tasks[9].

…but not on its own facts

“LLMs struggle to self-correct their responses without external feedback, and at times, their performance even degrades”[10].

Design rule

Give the checker something external: the source pack, a unit test, a hand calculation, a person. A generate-then-evaluate loop works best with “clear evaluation criteria”[1].

What the evidence says

Research agents, measured

Literature agent

PaperQA2

Precision 85.2% vs 73.8% for PhD-level humans on its question set; accuracy about the same (66.0% vs 67.7%). Still, 13.5% of cited statements in its articles were unsupported[12].

Automated science

The AI Scientist

Papers for under $15 each, by its authors’ account[13]. An independent test: 42% of experiments failed on coding errors, some papers had hallucinated numerical results[14].

Coding

Feeling vs measured

Early-2025: experienced developers took 19% longer with AI and believed they were 20% faster[15]. METR now calls those results out of date[16].

Agents can be very good. You still have to measure, because the output looks equally confident either way.

04

Integrity

How AI watermarks work, why AI-text detectors misfire, and what to do about your abstract.
Watermarking · at generation time

A text watermark is a statistical nudge

At each word, the previous token seeds a random split of the vocabulary into a green list and a red list of equal size; the model is nudged toward green[28].

A detector that knows the key counts green tokens and runs a z-test. At z > 4 the false-positive chance is 3 × 10−5[28].

Removing it from a long text takes rewriting roughly a quarter of the tokens or more[28].

Human text · about half green by chance
Watermarked text · mostly green

Illustration, not data.

Watermarking · in production

Real, deployed, and easy to wash out

Google

SynthID-Text

Watermarks Gemini’s text; in a test on nearly 20 million Gemini responses, users rated quality the same[29]. Open-sourced in Hugging Face Transformers[30]. SynthID also marks images, audio and video[31].

Limits

Edits weaken it

“Weakened by edits to the text, such as through LLM paraphrasing”[29]; scores “greatly reduced” by thorough rewriting or translation[30]. Enforcing it on open-weight models “is difficult”[29].

OpenAI

Built, still weighing

Has a text watermarking method it “continue[s] to consider”; less robust to translation or rewording with another model, and it “could stigmatize use of AI” by non-native English speakers[32].

A watermark can show a text came from one vendor’s model. Its absence proves nothing.

Detection · after the fact, without a key

AI-text detectors misfire, and not at random

61.3%

of TOEFL essays by non-native English writers labeled “AI-generated”, on average across seven detectors

97.8% were flagged by at least one detector; US eighth-graders’ essays were judged accurately[33].

OpenAI withdrew its own

Caught 26% of AI text, flagged 9% of human text; withdrawn July 20, 2023 “due to its low rate of accuracy”[34].

14 tools tested

“Neither accurate nor reliable”; obfuscation made them worse[35]. Paraphrasing defeats detectors and watermarks alike[36].

When the score is treated as proof

False accusations are a predictable failure mode

~750

papers a year at Vanderbilt that a 1% false-positive rate could mislabel, out of 75,000. It disabled the detector[42].

~6,000

misconduct cases at Australian Catholic University in 2024, about 90% AI-related; students were wrongly accused and ACU dropped the tool[43].

“Not the sole basis”

Turnitin’s own caution about its scores, quoted in that report[43]. Its sentence-level false-positive rate is about 4%[44].

We found no documented case of a researcher falsely flagged by a journal. The base rates say it will happen; be ready to show your work.

For your abstract

Your Mars Society abstract: show your work

The 2026 call

300 words, maximum

The 2026 form rejected abstracts over 300 words; the call asked for 150–300[50,51]. No 2027 call is posted yet, and we found no AI policy on its pages.

1 · Disclose

One sentence: which tool, for what, and that you checked it. Follow the strictest policy you might face[49,47].

2 · Keep your history

Write in a tool with version history; keep your prompts and drafts. That is your evidence if a score is wrong.

3 · Verify every citation

55% of GPT-3.5’s citations and 18% of GPT-4’s were fabricated[52]. Lawyers were fined $5,000 for fake ones[53].

Hands-on · prompts on the lecture page

Four steps, and on a free chat model, you are the loop

1 · Instruct

Role and rules: use only the source pack, cite every number, write “not in source pack” instead of guessing.

→

2 · Ground

Paste the source pack: eleven hazards, SI units, a link for each.

→

3 · Draft

Hazard | value [source] | subsystem | failure mode | DfR mitigation.

→

4 · Check, revise

A strict reviewer, in a new chat, checks each row against the pack. Revise until PASS.

Then open two source links by hand and confirm the values. Any free model works: Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, or a local model in Ollama.

Stretch goal · for anyone who wants code

The same loop, run by a program

# full script on the lecture page
draft = chat(DRAFTER, pack + "Draft the hazard table.")
for n in range(1, MAX_ROUNDS + 1):       # iteration
    report = chat(CHECKER, pack + draft)
    last = report.strip().splitlines()[-1]
    if "PASS" in last and "REVISE" not in last:  # selection
        break
    draft = chat(DRAFTER, pack + draft + report)
else:
    print("No PASS: check the draft by hand.")

Where to run it

Ollama on a laptop (Module 1, Lab A), or an Ollama app in the CyVerse Discovery Environment: a free account with a .edu address, then request VICE access in the User Portal[23,24].

How it talks to the model

Ollama’s REST API, POST /api/chat, standard-library Python only[19].

Beyond watermarks

Provenance metadata, and the EU’s marking rule

C2PA Content Credentials

Signed provenance

A manifest is “a cryptographically bound structure that records an asset’s provenance”. Can it be removed? “Yes it can.” And it cannot say whether content is true[70].

EU AI Act, Article 50

Machine-readable marks

From Aug. 2, 2026, providers must mark synthetic content in machine-readable form[71]; systems already on the market have until Dec. 2, 2026[71,72]. A voluntary Code of Practice explains how[73].
